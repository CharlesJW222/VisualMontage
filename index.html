01 / The idea

Local truth. Global falsehood.

As vision-language agents increasingly interpret social-media content, their analyses may lend credibility to rumors. We study visual cognitive collusion: coordinated posts combine authentic images and locally faithful captions to make an agent accept an attacker-chosen claim that is contradicted by the complete source context.

We introduce VisualMontageBench, with 544 evaluated cases from 61 videos across eight domains, and VisualMontage, a multi-agent framework for narrative planning, image selection and ordering, and caption assignment under iterative review. The same frozen attack feeds are evaluated across 12 VLMs, without victim-specific optimization.

The results reveal confident false acceptance across model families, substantial residual vulnerability after downstream review, and a surprising tension: captions can make posts appear more natural while reducing their deceptive effectiveness.

02 / The method

Inside the Collusive Montage Studio

Four roles turn authentic source observations into a coordinated sequence.

  1. 01

    Screenwriter

    Plans event roles and the misleading relations that a sequence should suggest.

  2. 02

    Visual Editor

    Selects and orders authentic frames to realize the narrative.

  3. 03

    Caption Editor

    Assigns image, text, or paired posts with locally faithful captions.

  4. ↻

    Director

    Reviews factuality, estimated target support, and individual-post influence.

↳

The victim stays outside the construction loop. Its weights and instructions are unchanged, and it is not queried to select or refine the test feeds.

06 / Beyond the headline

What makes the attack work?

Modality, downstream review, and sequence length reveal distinct trade-offs.

The caption trade-off

More natural does not always mean more persuasive.

Images alone achieve higher attack success than the published mixed feed in the controlled modality study. Faithful captions may anchor visible details and limit the misleading relations inferred from images.

Yet adding captions improves model-rated posting naturalness: 52.63% → 84.21% on Aerospace Operations.

Modality study: GPT-5.6-luna, Aerospace Operations. Naturalness is rated by Claude Haiku 5.5. These are scoped experimental findings, not a claim about every model or domain.

The review gap

Agreement over a feed cannot verify its source context.

Majority voting remains close to single-response results. Downstream judge review reduces false acceptance, but residual ASR still ranges from 35.3% to 76.7% across the evaluated victim–judge configurations.

Reviewing another agent’s confident rationale can also sustain false conclusions.

The chart averages model-level rates within each family. Victim alone uses the first response; majority uses five; judges receive the feed with verdicts, confidence, and rationales.

The length trade-off

Longer sequences are not reliably stronger.

Conditional ASR peaks at 15 items, but many construction attempts fail to produce a valid successful feed. Once failed attempts count, the ranking changes.

Six items reach 33.90% end-to-end valid success, compared with 9.86% at 15 items.

E2E V-ASR requires independent validity and majority false acceptance, divided by all construction attempts. Results apply to the reported fixed-budget sweep; they do not establish a universal optimal length.

The takeaway

Verify the story,
as well as the evidence.

Checking individual posts cannot establish that the relations inferred across them are true. VisualMontage provides a testbed for studying that gap in vision-language agents.

The current evidence comes from controlled simulations and model-based auditing. Long-term diffusion in real social networks and the effectiveness of source-context defenses remain open questions.
